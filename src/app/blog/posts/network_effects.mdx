I was reading about network effect and it shone a different light upon the fall of many Web3 projects. 
Firt things first: defintition. The network effect descibe a phenomenon where a 
product increases in value as more users join in. 

## Get the user side almost right...
For Web3, here's the mechanics of it: 
people use the protocol, they need to buy token, they use them to perform what the protocol offers 
(privacy, etc.), the token inflates in value. Fair to say, Web3 protocols understood that they to 
reach a critical mass of users to kickstart this effect. If no one uses the protocl, to one needs the token, 
the token is worthless. 

Web3 artificially created this critical mass by subsidizing users. 
You got incentivize to buy and hold the token, via ridicilously high yield rates, 
sometims over the 10% bar, and everyone was happy.

## Forget about the business side?
You think you got the user side covered? Great! Now, what are they're going to do with the token? 
Most protocols failed at that: they viewed themselves as facilitators, not as product makers. They outsourced that crirtical part to grant recipients. That's why we ended up with a flurry of trading platforms, wallets, and other staple products no one really needs. 

## No usage, no business
Missing the second part of the equation was the fatal mistake that sunk the company. A recent Token Terminal study found out that in 2025, only 10% of Web3 projects generated more than $1,000 a month in revenue. I fought hard to convince founders that they needed usage for their protocols, usage that went way beyond trading your token. 

It's a complex issue, that needs careful considering. What vertical are you going for? What problems are you uniquely positionned to solve? Do you have the ability or runway to solve it? You have to start thinking more like a regular business than a "disruptive" decentralised protocol. 

*That's a rule we've known since the 2000s: no usage, no business.*
