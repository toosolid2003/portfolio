I was reading about network effect and it shone a different light upon the fall of many Web3 projects. 
Firt things first: defintition. The network effect descibe a phenomenon where a 
product increases in value as more users join in. For Web3, here's the mechanics of it: 
people use the protocol, they need to buy token, they use them to perform what the protocol offers 
(privacy, etc.), the token inflates in value. Fair to say, Web3 protocols understood that they to 
reach a critical mass of users to kickstart this effect. If no one uses the protocl, to one needs the token, 
the token is worthless. 

Web3 artificially created this critical mass by subsidizing users. 
You got incentivize to buy and hold the token, via ridicilously high yield rates, 
sometims over the 10% bar, and everyone was happy until the incentives disappeared. 
Because they were incentivizing users but not usage, the sand castle fell apart in no time. 

**That's a rule we've known since the 2000s: no usage, no business. **
